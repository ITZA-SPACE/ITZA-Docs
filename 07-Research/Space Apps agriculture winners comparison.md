# ITZA outbuilds past winners but faces eligibility risk

Measured on the code, ITZA is technically ahead of every past award-level Space Apps agriculture project. It is the only one that computes a physically based water balance (FAO-56) from NASA inputs, tests that engine (781 passing tests), and publishes a validation against ground truth. The 2024 Best Use of Data winner (WaterWise) made one OpenET API call inside a formula with bugs in it. The 2024 Global Finalist OptiCrop passed point samples of SMAP and MODIS to a GPT-4 prompt. The Honorable Mention FarmVis showed its risk numbers from `random.uniform`, and the closest 2025 finalists generated their weather with `Math.random()`. ITZA's lead is narrower than its pitch suggests, though. Only NASA POWER, MODIS NDVI and NASADEM actually drive the decision. Its headline validation (78% agreement with USDA CDL) is only 11 points above a trivial "always crop" baseline of 67%. Its ranking contradicts the one field trial it checks against. Its 2050 replay fails on the live API for every real field. Judged on Impact, Creativity, Validity and Relevance with Presentation excluded, I estimate ITZA at roughly **3.5 / 4 / 3.5 / 4**, which is competitive with the past award winners. The largest risk to the project is not technical. Its published GitHub history was re-dated to start at the hackathon opening, while GitHub's own records show most of the work was done between Nov 11 and 13. That conflicts with the rule against pre-hackathon work and with the original-work attestation. The safe course is to disclose this to the Local Lead before the 23:59 deadline. In the remaining hours, the quickest gains on Validity and Relevance come from fixing the 2050 baselines and reporting results honestly against baselines.

## The pasted judging document is mostly accurate, but several quotes appear to be fabricated

The document gets the overall structure of judging right. Local or Universal judges nominate projects. NASA and partner subject-matter experts then select Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions. An executive committee picks **ten Global Awards**. Every level scores five criteria on a **1–5 scale** ([2026 Judging & Awards Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/)). Some details are out of date. The "1–3 nominees per event depending on size" rule comes from the **2022** guide ([2022 PDF](https://spaceapps-prod.s3.amazonaws.com/media/documents/Space_Apps_2022_Judging_and_Awards_Guide.pdf)) and is not in the current guide. "~40 finalists" was exactly right for 2023 and 2024 ([2023](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/awards/global-finalists/), [2024](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/awards/global-finalists/)), but 2025 had **45** ([2025](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/awards/global-finalists/)), and the guide never fixes a number. The yearly figures check out against NASA's releases. 2023 had **5,556 submissions** from 57,999 registrants ([NASA SMD](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners/)). 2024 had **9,996 submissions** from 93,520 registrants ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/)). 2025 had **11,500+ submissions** from 114,000+ participants, with 1,290+ Global Nominees ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-announces-2025-international-space-apps-challenge-global-winners/), [nominees](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/awards/global-nominees/)).

The eligibility claims are where reliable and unreliable statements sit side by side. The no-pre-work rule is real. The participant FAQ says verbatim: "Teams are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon" ([Participant FAQs](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/participant-faqs/)). Every submission must also tick a box confirming "the submitted project represents my team's original work" and listing all external resources in References ([Judging guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/)). The supposed official PDF instruction to "score it a 1 (Poor) and do not evaluate it further" could not be found anywhere. The guide's "Download as PDF" control just prints the web page. The 2026 guide gives only "Score of 5" descriptors. The real disqualifiers are the 30-second / 7-slide limits and the deadline ([Submission Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/)). I found no public guidance for judges on detecting pre-work and no documented disqualification for it. Official text describes the event as a "two-day hackathon", not a "48-hour" one. The quote that "using NASA data only for show… costs heavily under Validity/Relevance" is not official. It paraphrases the real **Relevance** criterion ("a core component… rather than a superficial addition"), and that wording does not appear under Validity. The "Hanoi judges advise" and "Ulaanbaatar judging page" citations also could not be verified. The Hanoi 2025 page exists but contains no judging text, and the Ulaanbaatar URLs return "Page Not Found" ([Hanoi](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/local-events/hanoi/)).

## One 2024 agriculture challenge produced two Global Winners, and neither validated anything

The 2024 challenge "Leveraging Earth Observation Data for Informed Agricultural Decision-Making" is the only direct predecessor of Field Shift. It drew **1,011 registered teams**. The site's own badge fields record **114 Global Nominees, 3 Global Finalists, 1 Honorable Mention and 2 Global Winners**, so this one challenge took two of the ten Global Awards ([Space Apps GraphQL API](https://api.spaceappschallenge.org/graphql); [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/)). The winners were:

- **GaamaRamma's WaterWise** (Universal Event, USA): Best Use of Data.
- **NVS-knot's 2plant** (Kyiv): Galactic Impact.

The non-winning finalist was **가지니/Gajini's OptiCrop** (Pangyo, South Korea), and the Honorable Mention was **FarmVis** (Houston). FarmVis was built by two Tompkins High School students and was "one of fewer than 20 projects to receive an Honorable Mention" ([My Neighborhood News](https://www.myneighborhoodnews.com/tompkins-high-school-students-recognized-at-nasas-global-app-design-challenge-for-innovative-sustainability-solution)). Among the 110 other nominees, five also won People's Choice: AgriTech Innovators, CropCops, Maquina del mal, Ometeotl and Stellar Agri-Innovator. Ometeotl's "Crop Connect" was featured by Meteomatics. Nominee write-ups are hidden from anonymous users, so how deep their data use went is unknown.

What these teams built differs a lot from what they claimed. **WaterWise** is a genuine weekend build: 78 commits by 4 authors between Oct 5 11:46 and Oct 6 19:35 CDT. Its only Earth-observation input is a live POST to the OpenET API (SSEBop model, Landsat-based, gridMET reference) for the user's polygon over the last 7 days. Precipitation comes from Open-Meteo. Water need is computed as `(Kc × ET − P) × area`. The code applies Kc to *actual* ET rather than reference ET, double-converts the polygon area, reuses past ET as the forecast, and shows soil-moisture values on the dashboard that no code path produces. It has no tests and a boilerplate README ([repo](https://github.com/gabe-engineers/nasa-hackathon-2024); [archived write-up](https://web.archive.org/web/20250207014509/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/gaamaramma/)). **2plant** has the deepest science of the group. It fuses SMAP and AMSR2 surface soil moisture through a reformulated exponential filter into 100 m root-zone moisture (error ~0.03 m³/m³), and adds SETMI thermal ET and Meteomatics ensembles to produce two-week planting-risk scores ([archived write-up](https://web.archive.org/web/20241120215349/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/nvs-knot/)). That science lives in a proprietary pipeline, though. The public repo is a single commit ("The app") of an existing product. It contains DB migrations dated Feb and Apr 2024 and only reads precomputed NetCDF files, with no SMAP or earthaccess code ([repo](https://github.com/yaroslav-prokipchyn/2plantORNOT2plant)). The team itself called it an MVP of Nave Analytics' product, which grew out of "2023-2024 experimental pilots". Its accuracy figures come from published literature, not from work done at the hackathon ([Silicon Prairie News](https://siliconprairienews.com/2025/02/nebraska-startup-wins-nasas-galactic-impact-award-in-the-2024-space-apps-challenge/)). Its live app no longer resolves.

**OptiCrop** is the only award-level predecessor that recommends crops, which makes it the closest analogue to ITZA. Its code really does read NASA granules: three days of SMAP L3 Enhanced, MODIS MOD13C1 NDVI, FLDAS ET and one IMERG granule. But the "recommendation engine" is a GPT-4 prompt that picks one of six crops. GRACE, ASTER, MOD16 and OSCAR are listed as references but never processed. The web app proxies to a LAN address (`192.168.0.6:8000`), and some repo commits predate the event by up to a week ([gajini-api](https://github.com/nasa-gajini/gajini-api), [gajini-web](https://github.com/nasa-gajini/gajini-web), [archived write-up](https://web.archive.org/web/20250408023319/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/gajini/?tab=project)). **FarmVis** embeds an ArcGIS "NASA soil moisture" web map as an iframe, so NASA data is display-only. Its flood and drought "probabilities" are `round(random.uniform(0, 100), 2)`. A DBSCAN step runs, but its output is only printed ([repo](https://github.com/sahusgupta/farmvis)).

Other years add less, because Space Apps had no Earth farm decision-support challenge before 2024. In 2025, farming appeared as an education-game challenge, "NASA Farm Navigators" ([challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/challenges/nasa-farm-navigators-using-nasa-data-exploration-in-agriculture/)). Its only finalist, **Zumorroda-X** ("Henry's Farm Adventures", Art & Technology winner), converts one IMERG HDF5 file offline. The flood level then replaces that data with a hard-coded 8×8 "testing" grid. Its random-forest crop model is never called by the game ([repo](https://github.com/esralsayed/test_farmy)). Agro Quest (Brazil) took that challenge's only Honorable Mention, and its project is not public. The two 2025 finalists closest to Field Shift came from "Will It Rain On My Parade?". **Stellar Minds' WeatherWise** labels seeded random forecasts as "NASA GPM IMERG" and "NASA SMAP", and its historical analysis actually uses the commercial Meteomatics API ([app.py](https://github.com/Amisa-2004/weather-wise/blob/main/backend/app.py)). **iNFINITY MISSION's Agricultural Weather Intelligence** describes a NASA POWER backend, but its public repo is one HTML file of `Math.random()` weather ([index.html](https://github.com/areejjmohammed/Agricultural_Weather_Intelligence/blob/main/index.html)). Earlier precedents include the 2021 Honorable Mention Ommatdium, a pest classifier whose promised NASA vegetation heatmap is just an OpenStreetMap map ([repo](https://github.com/raghavnautiyal/Farm-Free)). The 2021 Best Use of Science winner, Ani's Cuff, is the one past winner in this group whose model really used NASA EO inputs (NASADEM, TRMM, MCD12Q1, MOD13A1) ([project](https://2021.spaceappschallenge.org/challenges/statements/seeing-the-unseeable-viewing-bugs-from-space/teams/anis-cuff/project)).

Across all eight comparable projects, judges rewarded a clear decision a farmer could act on (a watering schedule, a "plant / don't plant" window, a playable story), not depth of data. None of them validated its outputs against ground truth during the hackathon.

## The rewritten commit history is a more serious problem than the pre-work itself

The rule is clear: "Teams are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon" ([FAQ](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/participant-faqs/)). The Terms set the start at November 14, 2026 ([Legal](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/)). ITZA's own docs record the start as "Sat Nov 14, 9:00" and list only accounts, a VPS and a shortlist as pre-event preparation (`docs/CHALLENGE_RULES_2026.md:15-16`, `docs/MVP_SCOPE.md:130-137`). GitHub's server-side records cannot be changed by rewriting git, and they show substantial earlier work:

- **Repository creation:** `ITZA-SPACE/itza` was created **2026-11-11T12:48:58Z**.
- **Pull requests:** **68 of 89 PRs were created and 63 merged** before 08:00Z on Nov 14 (09:00 local).
- **CI runs:** **242 of 340 Actions runs** happened before the start.
- **Original commits:** 173 of 205 predate the start. The first, "docs: add ITZA PRD v1", is dated Nov 11 05:34 +01:00 and was authored by Adel HB.
- **Dated artifacts:** the ML model card says it was trained Nov 12, and the hero bundles were captured Nov 13.

The branch now published as `main`/`dev` (`ff143b7`) is a disconnected copy of that history. The trees are byte-identical, but its 168 commits are re-dated to begin at **exactly 09:00:00 on Nov 14**, spaced 312–313 seconds apart in 164 of 167 intervals. The first commit is re-attributed from Adel HB to "wailbentafat". The evidence does not show who made the rewrite or why, so intent is not established, but the facts are unambiguous and visible to any reviewer who opens the PR or Actions tabs.

There is a fair case that enforcement has been inconsistent. **2plant won the 2024 Galactic Impact award** after openly presenting itself as the MVP of a startup's product built on 2023–2024 pilots. Its single-commit repo contains migrations from February and April 2024 ([write-up](https://web.archive.org/web/20241120215349/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/nvs-knot/); [repo](https://github.com/yaroslav-prokipchyn/2plantORNOT2plant)). OptiCrop reached Global Finalist with commits up to seven days before the event ([gajini-web](https://github.com/nasa-gajini/gajini-web)). The front-end repo of the 2021 winner Ani's Cuff was created a week before its event ([TsungTang/debugger](https://github.com/TsungTang/debugger)). No public disqualification for pre-work turned up. In practice, then, judges have tolerated prior work when it was declared. That precedent does not cover ITZA's situation. 2plant **disclosed** its prior work. ITZA's published history **conceals** it, and the team will have to tick a box attesting that the submission "represents my team's original work". A Local Lead who sees a history that starts exactly at the opening bell, next to PRs merged three days earlier, will treat it as an integrity issue, not a timing technicality. That outcome can cost more than any score, including risk to the team's standing and that of its members' institutions.

The recommendation is to **disclose to the Local Lead before submitting**. Tell them in writing what was built from Nov 11 to 13 (PRD, engine, science API, UI, ML training, bundles) and that the published history was re-dated. Restore the original history as the public record: it still exists in the local remote-tracking refs (`origin/main` tip `05ab14c`). Ask whether the project can compete, compete with the pre-work disclosed, or enter as non-competing. Update `docs/MVP_SCOPE.md` and the project page so they match the real timeline, and list pre-existing work in the References field. Do not submit the re-dated history, do not edit GitHub metadata, and do not describe the project as built entirely within the event. The 2plant precedent suggests honest disclosure may well be accepted, and concealment has no upside once the server timestamps are public.

## Conclusion

The past Space Apps agriculture winners set a low bar for data honesty and a high bar for decision clarity. A tool won Best Use of Data with one ET call and a broken area formula, and an Honorable Mention went to a project whose risk scores were random numbers. ITZA clears the honesty bar more convincingly than any project in this set. Its remaining exposure on Validity comes from evidence it published itself: a modest margin over a trivial baseline, and a trial it ranks the wrong way. Those findings help the project when they are explained and hurt it when a judge discovers them unannounced. On Relevance, cutting the NASA data table to the three products that actually drive the decision would likely raise the score more than adding a fourth.

The decisive variable is no longer the code. The 2plant precedent shows that disclosed prior work can win a Global Award. ITZA's history instead appears to hide the same kind of prior work. Disclosing now costs ITZA at most its eligibility for one round. If the re-dating is discovered after submission, its consequences reach well beyond this hackathon.
